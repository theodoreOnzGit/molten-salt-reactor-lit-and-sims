## U-233 operation

From p. x (PDF p. 12):

- **The first phase of MSRE operation ended** with "this six-month run" (the
  page gives no date for its end). Its objective was to demonstrate the feasibility of the
  concept on a small scale. The report says this was achieved: no corrosive
  attack above 1200 °F, stable fuel, and xenon removed rapidly.
- **The second phase began in August 1968.** The original uranium was removed
  from the fuel salt with gaseous F₂ in a small facility in the MSRE
  building: **219 kg of uranium in six days of fluorination**, loaded onto
  sodium fluoride absorbers.
- **The U-233 came from the Savannah River reactors.** It was converted to
  UF₄–LiF enriching salt in ORNL's Thorium-Uranium Recycle Facility, then
  added to the original carrier salt.
- **The MSRE became the world's first reactor to operate on U-233.** Power
  operation on U-233 ran **January–June 1969**.
- **Its nuclear characteristics with U-233 were close to the predictions,**
  and the reactor was quite stable.
- **One surprise:** much more gas entrained in the salt, which made the power
  noisy. A slight cut in pump speed removed the gas.
- **May 1969:** some carrier salt was distilled at the reactor, to show LiF
  and BeF₂ can be recovered from irradiated salt.
- **August 1969:** a second power period with U-233 began.
- **September 1969:** small amounts of PuF₃ were added to the fuel.

**A date in the report contradicts itself.** The page prints "in October 1969
the MSRE became the world's first reactor to operate on ²³³U" (checked on the
page image; it is printed, not an extraction error). The same paragraph puts
fluorination in August 1968 and power on U-233 from January 1969, so
criticality on U-233 must have been in **1968**. October 1968 is the likely
intended date. **Not confirmed in a second source:** ORNL-4396 (period ending
February 1969) should settle it.

## Why the breeder became one-fluid

From p. x (right column):

- **Two-fluid:** until about late 1967, ORNL's breeder work aimed at two-fluid
  systems. Graphite tubes would separate the uranium-bearing fuel salt from
  the thorium-bearing fertile salt. Proving that graphite can serve as piping
  under high fast-neutron irradiation would have needed years on a prototype.
- **Late 1967, two developments made one-fluid feasible:**
  1. demonstrated chemical steps for **extracting protactinium and uranium
     selectively into liquid bismuth** from a salt that also holds thorium;
  2. a **fertile blanket can be made within one salt** holding both uranium
     and thorium, by **reducing the graphite-to-fuel ratio in the outer part
     of the core**.
- **The outcome:** ORNL's studies showed a one-fluid, two-region breeder with
  fuel utilisation approaching the two-fluid designs and probably better
  economics. "Since the graphite serves only as moderator, the one-fluid
  reactor is more nearly a scaleup of the MSRE." The programme switched its
  emphasis to one-fluid.
